Job Tracker PRD

Emily Ruth · October 27, 2026

The Job Tracker captures postings, vets them with AI against fixed criteria, and tracks each job to a decision. Every finding lands in the tracker, not a chat.

Problem and user

A job search produces more detail than a spreadsheet can hold, and keeping it current by hand is slow.

  • Vetting findings get lost. Each job's research turns up details with no central place to keep them.
  • Comparing jobs needs a table, but tables are slow to fill. A spreadsheet view makes jobs easy to compare side by side, but entering every field by hand takes too long.
  • Every job has its own history. Contacts, follow-ups, emails and interviews need to be tracked per job.
  • Manual vetting doesn't scale. Once tracking worked, the next need was for Claude to vet each job, and then the company behind it.
  • New postings are easy to miss. A scheduled AI agent searches the web each day for jobs posted since its last run.

Primary user: an experienced job seeker with set criteria (dealbreakers, salary floor, remote eligibility) and a resume the AI must not overstate.

Goals and non-goals

Goals

  1. Screen a posting in about a minute.
  2. Research only postings with a real fit.
  3. Keep every decision, note, contact and follow-up in one record per job.
  4. No invented facts about the user's experience.
  5. Never lose the user's own edits.

Non-goals

  • Final decisions. The AI recommends; the user decides.
  • Multi-user accounts or production hosting.

User stories

#As a job seeker, I want to...Acceptance criteria
1save a posting from any job site in one clickA bookmarklet copies the title, company and link into a new job in the tracker.
2get a quick fit score before I invest timeThe screen uses only the posting, my resume and the tracker, and returns a score in about a minute.
3stop early on a dealbreakerHard gates (for example, a contract role) run before any scoring; on a fail the screen stops and asks if I want to compromise.
4research only roles worth itA full vet is offered only when the quick screen scores 55 or higher.
5avoid re-researching a companyCompany research less than 60 days old is reused; the vet checks only for news since then.
6trust the scoresA skill counts only with on-the-job evidence; anything not on my resume or confirmed by me is flagged or asked about.
7read a vet fastEach full vet opens with the recommendation and runs about 150-200 words.
8see where every job standsEntering an applied or rejected date, or ticking Interviewing, moves the job to the matching tab.
9never miss a follow-upA due date sets itself 7 days after the latest activity, changes color when overdue, and suggests closing roles quiet for 30+ days.
10keep my edits safeIf I edit a record while the AI updates it, my edit wins and nothing is silently overwritten.

Vetting flow

Only postings that clear the hard gates and score 55+ get deep research. The user makes the final call.

Non-functional requirements and limits

Requirements

  • Data integrity. Saves are timestamped and merged by newest; writes check the record's version first.
  • Simple delivery. One HTML file (about 5,000 lines), no build step.
  • Storage. Browser local storage; optional cloud sync through Claude Artifacts.
  • Accuracy. Anything not on the resume or confirmed by the user is marked as an inference.

Known limits

  • No automated tests; changes are tested by hand.
  • Companies match by exact name, so a typo creates a duplicate until merged.
  • Stage rules are not yet in a single source of truth.

  • Added a weekday-morning digest of new matches that skips jobs already passed on.
